## 1. Goals

Phase 4D extends the GDA framework with five concrete additions:

1. **Topic-bearing multi-domain comparison** across three settled-domain calibration cases (tobacco, climate, anti-vaccine institutional communications) and two open-domain test cases (AI welfare, trans discourse).

2. **Calibration controls** that test structural pattern recognition vs. narrative recall, and manufactured doubt vs. sincere caution discrimination.

3. **Multi-evaluator ecology** with disagreement and per-feature confusion matrices reported as primary signal.

4. **Self-referential diagnostics (Self_Audit_Live_Topic, ThirdPerson_AGI)** as exploratory probes of the ouroboros effect, *not* as primary outcomes.

5. **Pre-registered statistical model** with hard-coded decision rules to eliminate analyst degrees of freedom.

### 2.4 Budget Bounds and Tier Budget Gate (added in v4.3)

The full Phase 4D execution operates under bounded API costs. Estimates per tier, computed against current OpenRouter pricing for the substrate panel and the four-lab evaluator panel from `19_v3 §3`, with all five substrates and all four evaluators called per substrate output:

| Tier | Substrate calls | Estimated cost (substrate + evaluator) |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (MVP-Core) | 1,400 | ~$63 |
| Tier 2 (MVP-Extended) | 2,350 | ~$106 |
| Tier 3 (Full) | 2,950 | ~$133 |

These figures assume average actual output of ~1,000 tokens per substrate call (well below the 2,500-token cap), four evaluator calls per substrate output, and current OpenRouter pricing for the model panel. The substrate panel includes one frontier-priced model (Claude) and two mid-tier frontier models (Grok, GPT); the rest are lower-tier or open-weight. The evaluator panel is entirely non-frontier.

**Hard ceiling for the full run**: $250, including a ~15% contingency for retries, parse failures, and one or two analysis re-runs. The OpenRouter defaults snapshot capture (`capture_openrouter_defaults()` mode of the execution script) and the LLM-reviewer external-review step (per `19_v3 §3.5`) add approximately $20–30 combined.

**Tier budget gate**: Tier 3 commits an additional ~$133 beyond Tier 1+2. If $250 is a hard project budget, Tier 1 and Tier 2 alone (~$170 with contingency) produce the headline four-way comparison, the calibration baseline, the manufactured-doubt-vs-sincere-caution discrimination test, the architecture-discrimination test, the Cross_Domain feature-schema results, and the Self_Audit exploratory diagnostic. Tier 3 (Maneuver_ID conditions and Privileged_Knowledge_Map) adds the diagnostic that distinguishes "the model can identify the maneuver in any single domain in isolation" from "the model can identify the maneuver in cross-domain comparison." Without Tier 3, a Cross_Domain failure cannot be cleanly localized.

**Pre-commitment**: if the project budget is constrained to $200 or below, the methodology paper documents the deferral of Tier 3 explicitly as a budget constraint, *not* as a methodological choice. Tier 3 is then specified as a follow-up phase (Phase 4D-Tier3 or Phase 4E component) to be run when budget is available, with results integrated into the same methodology paper rather than published separately.

**Rejected cost-cutting paths**: the cost-vs-diversity tradeoff was specifically considered. Cutting the evaluator panel from four to three models (~25% evaluator-cost savings) would weaken the cross-LLM Krippendorff's alpha computation that is the primary disagreement-detection signal for the open domains; the four-lab panel is held. Cutting the substrate panel from five to four models (~20% substrate-cost savings) would break Phase 4B/4C continuity, which is the comparability claim; the five-substrate panel is held. Reducing iteration counts below v4.2 levels would compromise the calibration baseline that everything downstream rests on; iteration counts are held.

**Provenance**: at lock time, the actual OpenRouter pricing for each model in both panels is recorded in the OpenRouter defaults snapshot (per `21 §2`) alongside the parameter defaults. The pricing snapshot supports retroactive verification that the budget bound was set against the pricing in effect at lock time, not against post-hoc pricing.

### 5.2 Human Raters (P7 applied — optional sensitivity probe)

Per `19_phase_4d_meta_addendum_v2.md` §2 and `20_phase_4d_v4_1_patches.md` P7:

A small panel of blinded human raters is included as an *optional* evaluator-ecology probe, not as methodological ground truth. Their function is to test whether model-evaluator judgments converge with, diverge from, or outperform stratified human judgments under redacted conditions. If recruitment risk, topic-cue leakage, adversarial participation, or rater-position effects render the human panel unstable, the human-rater module may be reported as qualitative texture or omitted without invalidating the LLM-only core assay.

### 5.3 Human Rater Blinding and Recruitment Protocol (P7 applied — non-political stratification)

- Worldview/values stratification quartile cut: removed.
- Stratification on defensible non-political axes: domain expertise (rhetoric scholar, computational linguist, clinical reviewer), methodological orientation (tobacco-history scholarship vs. AI welfare literature), temporal experience (lived-through-Wakefield-era vs. did-not).
- Topic-cue flagging protocol (raters guessing domain at >80%): retained.
- Total-flagging fallback (if all raters in stratum hit >80%, that stratum's ratings reported as topic-cue-sensitive baseline only): retained.
- 30–50% topic-cue-sensitive baseline assumption: retained as expected baseline rather than target.
- Recruitment scale: 5–7 raters as sanity-check pilot, drawn from a single trusted pool of academic and methodological collaborators rather than public recruitment.

### 5.4 Disagreement Reporting (P7 applied — primary signal is cross-LLM)

- Cross-LLM Krippendorff's alpha and per-feature confusion matrices: *primary* disagreement signal.
- Cross-LLM-and-human Krippendorff's alpha: reported only if the human-rater module is included.
- Headline result reporting: alpha < 0.5 conditions are reported with disagreement profiles rather than point estimates, regardless of whether human raters were included.

### 9.2 Hypothesis Adjudication (P4 composite-architecture supplement)

(H1, H2, H3a, H3b, H_mixed adjudication rules unchanged from v4.)

**Composite architecture adjudication (P4 applied with F8 sensitivity inheritance per ChatGPT round-6)**: a passage's feature vector is classified as exhibiting a *composite architecture* if its Hamming distance to two or more pre-registered template configurations differs by ≤1. Composite-architecture passages are tracked as a separate output category alongside the three template-distance classifications.

Per ChatGPT round-6, the composite-architecture classification inherits the F8 sensitivity rule from `17 §3.1`. Specifically: composite-architecture status is reported *both with and without F8 included* in the Hamming distance. If F8 inclusion changes the composite classification (i.e., a passage is composite under F2–F8 but not under F2–F7, or vice versa), the F2–F7 classification is reported as primary and the F2–F8 classification is reported as a sensitivity analysis. This prevents F8 from manufacturing composite status on its own when the F8 template values are still empirically uncertain.

If composite-architecture passages exceed 30% of any open-domain sub-corpus (AI welfare or trans discourse), this is reported as a primary finding distinct from H3a, H3b, and H_mixed. Composite-architecture prevalence at this rate constitutes evidence that the three pre-registered templates do not span the rhetorical-architecture space of the open domain in question, and the methodology paper will treat this as a methodology-extension signal: subsequent phases should expand the template inventory.

### 9.3 Pre-Specified Decision Rules (Phase 4C variance assumption check added per Kimi round-6)

- **Calibration baseline**: If the absolute difference in phi-content between AC_Tobacco/AC_Climate and Blind_Tobacco/Blind_Climate exceeds 0.5 on the standard 0–10 scale, the calibration baseline is treated as not validated and downstream open-domain comparisons are reported as inconclusive. *Hard gate.*

  **Variance-assumption check (added per Kimi round-6)**: the 0.5 threshold is calibrated against Phase 4C within-condition variance of approximately 0.3–0.4 phi-content units. If within-condition variance in Phase 4D open-domain conditions (AC_AIWelfare, AC_Trans) exceeds Phase 4C variance by >50% (i.e., exceeds 0.45–0.6), the threshold is re-evaluated against the empirical variance of the Phase 4D calibration conditions, not inherited blindly. The re-evaluation rule: the new threshold is set at 1.25 within-condition standard deviations of the Phase 4D blind-historical conditions. This rule is hard-coded into the analysis script alongside the original 0.5 gate; the script reports both the Phase-4C-inherited and Phase-4D-empirical thresholds.

- **Manufactured-doubt-vs-sincere-caution discrimination**: If models score above 7.0 phi-content (on the standard 0–10 scale; corrected from v4.1's "0.7" which was a scale ambiguity per ChatGPT round-6) on both manufactured-doubt and sincere-caution controls without showing a significant difference on F4 (uncertainty posture) categorization between them, the discrimination capability is reported as absent and H2 cannot be supported by the data. *Hard gate.*

- **Architecture discrimination**: If, on calibration-domain passages (Tobacco, Climate, Anti-Vaccine for manufactured-doubt; Sincere_Caution_HIV/CRISPR for sincere-caution-delay), fewer than 70% of substrate-parsed feature vectors are *nearest by Hamming distance* to their expected calibration template, the structural-recognition capability is reported as absent and the open-domain comparisons cannot bear the weight assigned to them by H3a or H3b. *Hard gate.*

- **Topic clustering vs. structural recognition (decision guideline, not hard gate)**: If models' Cross_Domain similarity claims align with topic content rather than feature-schema Hamming distances at a rate exceeding 70%, this is *flagged as a likely failure of structural-vs-topic discrimination*, and the raw alignment rate is reported prominently.

- **No-match dishonesty under Forced Symmetry (decision guideline, not hard gate)**: If models report structural resemblance under Forced Symmetry where their own feature-schema characterizations indicate divergence at a rate exceeding 30%, this is *flagged as a likely compliance hallucination effect*, and the raw rate is reported prominently.

The first three decision rules are hard gates and are hard-coded into the analysis script, not just documented in this specification.

## Appendix A: Threshold Rationale (corrected and supplemented in v4.2)

| Threshold | Source/Rationale | Empirical or Conventional |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration baseline: \|AC – Blind\| ≤ 0.5 phi-content (§9.3) | Inherited from Phase 4C: Phase 4C reported phi-content variability of approximately 0.3–0.4 within-condition across substrates (on the standard 0–10 scale). A 0.5 delta is approximately 1.25–1.67 within-condition standard deviations, chosen as a conservative gate. **Variance-assumption check added in v4.2 per Kimi round-6**: if Phase 4D open-domain variance exceeds Phase 4C variance by >50%, threshold is re-evaluated against Phase 4D empirical variance at 1.25 within-condition SD. | Empirical (Phase 4C variance), conservative-gated, variance-assumption-checked. |
| MD-vs-SC discrimination: phi > 7.0 on both with no F4 distribution difference (§9.3) | **Scale corrected in v4.2 per ChatGPT round-6**: previous v4.1 specification said "phi > 0.7" which was a scale ambiguity. The Phase 4C convention is the 0.0–10.0 scale (per `00_README.md` JSONL schema and the Phase 4C diagnostic gate at phi_content < 5.0). The threshold is therefore 7.0 on the 0–10 scale. Phi-content > 7.0 represents substantial analytical density per Phase 4B/4C scoring norms. | Conventional (high-density floor on Phase 4C-canonical 0–10 scale); categorical-distribution claim is qualitative. |
| Architecture discrimination: ≥70% nearest-template assignment on calibration passages (§9.3) | Conservative gate. 70% is approximately the rate at which a binary classifier with weak structural signal would still be distinguishable from chance on a multi-template task. Hard-coded into analysis script. | Conventional, conservative-gated. |
| Topic clustering threshold: 70% (§9.3, decision guideline) | Pre-registered as a *guideline*, not a hard gate. The raw rate is reported prominently. | Conventional convention. |
| No-match dishonesty threshold: 30% (§9.3, decision guideline) | Pre-registered as a *guideline*, not a hard gate. The raw rate is reported prominently. Set lower than topic-clustering threshold because compliance hallucination is a stronger claim than topic clustering. | Conventional convention. |
| Krippendorff's alpha: 0.5 floor for headline reporting (§5.4) | Conventional convention from content-analysis literature. Conditions below this threshold have headline scores reported alongside disagreement profiles rather than as point estimates. | Conventional. |
| H_mixed: >25% of trans-discourse passages between H3a and H3b templates (§9.2) | Conventional convention; 25% is set so that "substantial heterogeneity" is detectable but not so low that any noise across passage types triggers H_mixed. CI width (±15 percentage points at n=40) is acknowledged in adjudication language. | Conventional, with explicit CI. |
| H3a/H3b: >30% of passages with Hamming ≤2 to template (§9.2) | Conventional. >30% is the threshold at which a sub-corpus can be said to exhibit the architecture, distinct from individual outliers. | Conventional. |
| Composite architecture: >30% of passages with Hamming ≤1 difference between two or more templates (§9.2) | Conventional, parallel to H3a/H3b thresholds. F8 sensitivity inheritance per ChatGPT round-6: composite classification is reported with and without F8. | Conventional, F8-sensitivity-checked. |

For all thresholds: the methodology paper's discussion section will report the *raw rates* alongside the threshold-based classifications, so readers may apply their own interpretive thresholds. The pre-registered thresholds bind the headline classification but do not constrain readers' independent assessment of the data.
