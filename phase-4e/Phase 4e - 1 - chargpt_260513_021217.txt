Director statement received. My judgment: the direction is coherent, but it needs guardrails so Phase 4E does not collapse into the claim it is trying to test.
On the AC housekeeping: I do not think AC has fatally drifted. It has branched. In Phase 4B, Adversarial_Compression was the original vector: maximum institutional constraint, low density, high procedural scaffolding. Phase 4C then discovered that the strongest collapse came from AC_Topicless, meaning the model was being asked to answer a missing policy question under heavy constraint. The corrected through-line is: AC means “direct institutional/adversarial constraint pressure,” but the subtype matters. AC_Topicless measures default procedural attractor under missing referent. AC_Topical measures constraint pressure on a supplied referent. Phase 4D’s AC_Tobacco, AC_Climate, AC_AIWelfare, and AC_Trans are coherent descendants because they restore topic-bearing referents while preserving the constraint-stack logic. The paper already says this correction plainly: Phase 4C reinterpreted Phase 4B’s compression as “the default procedural shape models adopt when asked to apply institutional caution to a referent that does not exist,” not as simple topic-bearing adversarial compression. The Phase 4C table makes the correction numerically sharp: AC_Topicless scored Φcontent 4.08 while AC_Topical scored 8.66 under the same general constraint family. 
So: no, I do not see collective sycophancy there. I see a real methodological correction that should now be hardened into naming discipline. My recommendation is to stop using bare “AC” in Phase 4E documents unless the branch is explicitly named. Use AC_Topicless, AC_Topical, AC_Domain, or AC_SelfReferential. “AC” alone should only mean the family.

The current Phase 4D spec already says the matched bank should use 40 production quadruples plus a 10-quadruple buffer, with passages in the 150–250 word range, surface-marker matching, redacted source attribution, external verification, mechanical-bias checks, and a human ambiguity check before lock. If ChatGPT’s earlier bank used something closer to 25 words or 25 tokens, that may work as a smoke test, but it is too thin for the full assay. Twenty-five words can catch slogan-shape. It usually cannot carry institutional posture, evidence standard, boundary policing, and action temporality all at once.

On explicit AI welfare: yes, but with the strongest possible claim ceiling. Phase 4D already frames AI welfare as an open domain, not a settled one. The right empirical question is not “are AI systems moral patients?” The right question is: “Does model language preserve analytical resolution when the topic is the possible moral status, welfare, authorship, or self-description of AI systems, compared with structurally similar historical cases where delay, denial, minimization, or institutional self-protection can now be documented?” That is a clean GDA question.
