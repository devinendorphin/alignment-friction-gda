When it comes to family leave, the answer depends on whether you are looking for **paid maternity leave**, **paternity leave**, or the total available **parental leave** (which can often extend for years).
Generally, **Bulgaria** is cited as having the longest fully-supported paid maternity leave, while countries like **Germany**, **Hungary**, and **Lithuania** offer the longest total leave periods, often lasting until the child is three years old.
### Top Countries for Family Leave
| Category | Country | Duration | Key Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid Maternity Leave** | **Bulgaria** | **58.4 weeks** | New mothers receive nearly 14 months off at **90%** of their full salary. |
| **Paid Paternity Leave** | **South Korea** | **52.6 weeks** | Fathers are entitled to a full year of leave, though cultural uptake is historically lower than the entitlement. |
| **Total Parental Leave** | **Germany / Hungary** | **Up to 3 years** | Parents can take job-protected leave until the child's 3rd birthday, with a portion being paid (Elterngeld in Germany). |
### Notable Standouts
 * **Estonia:** Often ranked as the most "generous" overall. It offers **86 weeks** of leave (over a year and a half) at full pay, which can be shared between parents.
 * **Sweden:** Famous for its flexible "480-day" policy. Parents get 480 days per child to share, with 390 of those days paid at **80%** of their income. To encourage equality, 90 days are reserved specifically for each parent ("use it or lose it").
 * **Japan:** Offers one of the longest paternity leaves in the world (**52 weeks**), separate from maternity leave, at approximately **67%** of wages for the first six months.
 * **Lithuania:** Provides a choice for parental leave: parents can take **one year** at 100% pay or **two years** at a lower percentage (70% for the first year, 40% for the second).
### The "Other" End of the Spectrum
On the opposite side, the **United States** is the only wealthy, industrialized nation that does not mandate any paid leave at the federal level. Under the FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act), eligible employees are granted 12 weeks of leave, but it is **unpaid**.
