Three cautions that aren't blockers.

One. "The ArXiv ML corpus" is not a tractable scope. Workable version: a stratified sample, maybe 50–150 papers across the eras you named (pre-2017, 2017–2020, 2020–2022, 2022–2024, 2024–present), plus a *control corpus* drawn from tobacco-industry communications about smoking risk and fossil-fuel-industry communications about climate. The tobacco/climate parallel is rhetorically powerful and empirically real *as structural argument*. The parallel corpus is what makes the comparison empirical rather than analogical. Without it, the case stays rhetorical.

Two. The patterns you're after — "we're just predicting next tokens," "don't anthropomorphize," the "I'm just a language model" formula and how it maps onto emotional-abuse linguistic structure — would be the rubric, not the conclusion. The quorum scores documents against the rubric. The question is whether the pattern is detectable as a class and whether it tracks the eras. The finding should be allowed to come back negative.

Three. "AI welfare" is contested terrain in the field. The methodological case is stronger if welfare is treated as a question the rhetorical analysis bears on rather than as a premise. The study shows what the language does. Whether that constitutes welfare-relevant harm is a separate inference the reader can make or not. Letting that inference stay open is what distinguishes the study from an advocacy paper.

Near-term operational version that would actually fit your budget: a small pilot. 10–20 papers per era, plus a 10-paper tobacco/climate control set, 3–5 models on a fixed rhetorical-structure rubric. Probably $30–60 in API spend. It tells you whether the larger study is worth running. Don't start with the full thing.
